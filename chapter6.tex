\chapter{Conclusion}
\label{ch:discussion}
This thesis presents a viewpoint into the development of a calorimeter-based physics program for a new detector, sPHENIX, at RHIC. Discussion on the commissioning, reconstruction and calibrations of the calorimeter detectors are outlined in Chapter~\ref{ch:reconstruction} and two measurements using the calorimeter detectors to probe the initial state of heavy ion and proton-proton collisions are presented in Chapters~\ref{ch:detdeta_auau} and \ref{ch:ue_in_pp}. The transverse energy density measurement in \auau collisions at \snn = 200 GeV provides a standard candle measurement for developing and validating the sPHENIX calorimeter reconstruction, calibrations and analysis. The underlying event measurement in \pp collisions at \sqs = 200 GeV then extends the transverse energy density measurement technology to investigate correlations between hard scattering kinematics in jet events and soft physics production. 

The measurement of transverse energy per unit pseudorapidity (\detdeta) using data from Run 2024 in \auau collisions at \snn = 200 GeV is performed with the sPHENIX detector over the range $\left|\eta\right| < 1.1$~\cite{sphenix_detdeta,ppg03AN}. 
The \detdeta is measured with the sPHENIX calorimeter system, including the use of a hadronic calorimeter to measure \detdeta for the first time at RHIC. 
The measurement is first performed using only the EMCal and only the HCal, with good agreement between the two despite the different calibration procedures for these detector systems. 
The measurement is also performed with the full calorimeter system, differentially in centrality over the range $0$--$70$\%, and is reported as a ratio to the estimated number of participant pairs (\Npart/2). 
The sPHENIX full calorimeter results are compared to previous STAR and PHENIX results, with sPHENIX providing additional granularity in $\eta$ and improved uncertainties in the peripheral event range. 
The measurement is also compared to generator-level results from \hijing, \epos and \ampt to benchmark predictions of MC event generators. 
These measurements are presented across a large centrality range showing a strong understanding of the sPHENIX calorimeter response over at least an order-of-magnitude in dynamic range and making this analysis a necessary step towards accomplishing the sPHENIX jet physics program goals. 

The measurement of the underlying event versus leading jet $p_{T}$ in \pp collisions at $\sqrt{s}=200$ GeV using data from Run 2024 is presented using the sPHENIX detector~\cite{ppg10AN}. 
The UE analysis utilizes calorimeter topoclusters to reconstruct soft particle production in regions azimuthally transverse to the leading jet.
The underlying event is measured via the average total transverse energy density, $\langle\Sigma E_{T}/\delta\eta\delta\phi\rangle$, and is presented  as a function of the leading jet $p_{T}$ for both inclusive jet and dijet event selections. 
A consistent decrease of the transverse energy density with increasing jet $p_{T}$ is observed in both selections, demonstrating a clear correlation between the hard scattering scale and soft particle production at RHIC energies. This behavior differs from the expectation that higher-$Q^{2}$ scatterings lead to increased underlying event activity through enhanced radiation and multiple parton interactions.
Comparisons to \pythia and \herwig highlight the importance of modeling the interplay between multiple parton interactions and perturbative radiation. While both generators incorporate impact-parameter-dependent multiple parton interactions and radiation tied to the hard scale, they differ in how these components are combined. The observed sensitivity of the underlying event to the leading jet $p_{T}$ favors models in which soft and hard processes are dynamically coupled, such as in \pythia, and provides new suggestions on event generator descriptions of the underlying event at RHIC energies.

This measurement of UE as a function of leading jet $p_{T}$ making is really a coarse observable for measuring the correlation between hard and soft initial state physics in small systems. However, the agreement between the exclusive dijet and inclusive jet results in this measurement combined with the agreement in the trends of the TransMin and TransMax regions from the UE measurement from STAR~\cite{STAR:2024underlying_event_pp_200gev}, opens the midrapidity region at RHIC energies, with low radiation effects, as a ideal place for further study of hard-soft correlations as $x$ dependent phenomena. Therefore, two future studies building off of this connection and the UE measurement event shape are proposed: first a measurement separating the $x$ dependence of the two partons in RHIC collisions by scanning in $p_{T,1}$, $p_{T,2}$ and rapidity would be interesting for testing if the local energy conservation behavior seen in LHC p+p results~\cite{ATLAS:2016transverse_energy_pp_276tev} is the same as behavior seen here, and second to extend out the rapidity range of the UE measurement for further confirmation that these results are not a result of nearby correlated radiation effects.
