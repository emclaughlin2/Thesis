This chapter describes the measurement of the underlying event in inclusive jet and exclusive dijet events in p+p collisions at $\sqrt{s} = 200$ GeV using the sPHENIX calorimeter system. Measurements of the underlying event in p+p collisions probe the interplay between the hard scattering and soft particle production. 
The high-statistics p+p dataset collected by sPHENIX during RHIC Run 2024 allows for UE measurements at higher leading jet $p_{T}$ than previously accessible at RHIC, probing the large parton momentum fraction regime, where the interplay between hard and soft processes is particularly interesting.
It is also interesting to connect these results from underlying event measurements to studies of hard-soft correlations due to initial state effects, like energy conservation or color fluctuation effects, to see if these initial state effects are accessible using the UE measurement shape at RHIC energies, but may have been subdominant to radiation effects in LHC results at midrapidity.

At both the LHC and RHIC, the underlying event has been measured in p+p collisions as a function of the leading jet $p_{T}$ using the event shape introduced in Section~\ref{sec:underlying_event_measurements} (Figure \ref{fig:UEeventshape}). 
Results from the LHC at energies $>1$ TeV show consistent results that the UE rises as a function of leading jet $p_{T}$, which is expected from a simple picture of the underlying event where harder collisions produce more initial- and final-state radiation and are more central collisions thereby having more multiple parton interactions~\cite{pythia8}. An example of these results are shown from the ATLAS experiment at 7 TeV~\cite{ATLAS:2014underlying_event_jets_7tev} in Figure~\ref{fig:ue_pp_atlas}. 
However, when an exclusive dijet selection is applied, requiring back-to-back dijets with no third jet in the event, the ATLAS results show the opposite trend, with the UE decreasing with increasing leading jet $p_{T}$. 
This event-selection dependence at the LHC hints that this exclusive dijet requirement selects for events with lower ISR/FSR contributions, making the results more similar to UE measurements separated in rapidity from the hard scattering, and possibly exposing an underlying anti-correlation between the hard scattering scale and soft production.

Results from STAR at RHIC energies of 200 GeV~\cite{STAR:2024underlying_event_pp_200gev} (Figure \ref{fig:ue_pp_star}) show a similar trend to the LHC exclusive dijet results, where the UE decreases slightly with increasing leading jet $p_{T}$. It is argued that the decrease in the UE with increasing leading jet $p_{T}$ is consistent with energy conservation behavior. Importantly, the UE measurements in the TransMax and TransMin regions is found to be fairly consistent with one another. 
This suggests that ISR/FSR contributions, which are more likely to be localized and therefore in only one region of the transverse region, are a subdominant effect at RHIC than at LHC. This raises the question of whether the anti-correlation observed at RHIC is driven by the same initial state mechanisms seen in p+A collisions rather than by radiation effects.

Hard-soft correlation studies in p+A collisions provide additional evidence for initial state origins of this anti-correlation. Studies in p+Au collisions at RHIC also use the event shape shown in Figure \ref{fig:UEeventshape} to estimate the underlying event and see a similar trend of an anti-correlation between the leading jet $p_{T}$ and the underlying event for p+Au events~\cite{star_ue_pAu} attributed to parton x or $Q^2$ initial state effects, rather than radiation effects, due to the correlation spanning multiple units of rapidity. 
p+Pb studies at the LHC also see an anti-correlation between the leading-jet $p_{T}$ and the event activity measured in the forward region with a continuous trend as a function of the proton parton $x$, which is attributed to initial state color fluctuation effects in the proton~\cite{ATLAS:2024dijet_centrality_pPb_816tev}. 
A followup ATLAS study in p+p collisions at 2.76 TeV finds that the anti-correlation between forward event activity and hard scattering kinematics can be attributed to energy conservation effects, but importantly these effects are localized to the target proton and are absent in the projectile proton~\cite{ATLAS:2016transverse_energy_pp_276tev}. This asymmetry rules out energy conservation as the explanation for the $R_{cp}$ suppression in p+Pb, leaving color fluctuations as the preferred interpretation. 

These results in p+p and p+A systems suggest that the UE anti-correlation at RHIC and in exclusive dijet events at the LHC could reflect initial state physics, either energy conservation or color fluctuation effects, while the rising UE in inclusive LHC events is dominated by radiation contributions. 
The UE in p+p collisions at $\sqrt{s} = 200$ GeV is measured as the total transverse region energy density as a function of leading jet $p_T$ for both inclusive jet and exclusive dijet event selections, and the results are compared directly to the RHIC and LHC measurements described above. 
The UE measurement at RHIC energies is also extended to higher parton $x$ and measured using a total energy measurement, rather than a particle multiplicity measurement, better constraining the argument of initial state effects seen in the UE measurement, such as energy conservation, at RHIC energies.
