\subsection{Calorimeter Tower Reconstruction}
\label{sec:towerenergyreco}

The \detdeta analysis uses calorimeter tower energy and $\eta$ position information in order to reconstruct and measure the transverse energy per unit pseudorapidity, \detdeta, in Au+Au collisions at $\sqrt{s_{NN}}$ = 200 GeV.
The calorimeter tower energy information is reconstructed using the nominal calorimeter reconstruction, calibration and hot tower masking procedures outlined in Section~\ref{sec:nominal_calorimeter_reconstruction} and the $\eta$ position of each tower is determined on an event-by-event basis using the vertex position of the collision.

\subsubsection{Tower Energy Reconstruction and Calibration Details}

The \detdeta analysis uses the central production of calorimeter data in which waveforms are processed using the default reconstruction chain (Section~\ref{sec:nominal_calorimeter_reconstruction}).
The EMCal, IHCal, and OHCal tower energies are reconstructed separately using the same default reconstruction chain and are individually calibrated to the electromagnetic energy scale using the calibration procedures outlined in Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.
Additionally, cross checks for the hadronic calorimeter sampling fractions were performed to verify the sampling fraction for the individual calorimeter towers and determine any $\eta$ dependence in the sampling fraction. 
There is a very minor $\eta$ dependence in the sampling fraction of HCal towers observed in simulation particle gun studies. 
However, it was determined that for this analysis, the $\eta$-independent IHCal and OHCal sampling fraction values determined from simulation particle gun studies can be used, since the HCal absolute energy scale calibration is determined on the scintillator EM energy level and the same sampling fraction was applied to both simulation and data. 
Therefore, this sampling fraction value applied in both data and MC cancels out in the correction step when extracting measurements of the truth level \detdeta. 
Studies of the HCal sampling fraction are detailed in Appendix~\ref{sec:SamplingFrac}. 
Furthermore, the inclusion of realistic support structures into the sPHENIX \geant simulation was found to be necessary for accurately simulating the in-flow and out-flow of collision particles in the forward $\eta$ regions of the calorimeters. 
The detailed studies of the impact of the support structures for this analysis can be found in Appendix~\ref{sec:SupportStruct}.

\subsubsection{Hot Tower Masking}
Hot tower identification and masking based on waveform fitting results and tower-by-tower energy spectra shape are applied prior to analysis as outlined in the nominal calorimeter reconstruction details in Section~\ref{sec:nominal_calorimeter_reconstruction}.
To ensure the same detector acceptance is applied to both data and simulation, the hot tower mask created for each calorimeter layer in data is applied in both simulation and data.
Figure~\ref{fig:bad_tower_masks_detdeta} shows the full set of hot tower masks used in the \detdeta analysis.
These masks are applied independently for each calorimeter layer, EMCal, IHCal and OHCal, and the same mask is applied to both simulation and data.
Additionally, these masks are determined run-by-run and are applied independently to both nominal and variation runs used in the analysis to investigate acceptance effects on the \detdeta measurement. 
Following the application of the above calibration steps and these hot tower masks, the calorimeter tower energy values are ready to be used in the analysis procedure to determine the reconstructed \detdeta and correction factors for each calorimeter layer measurement.

\subsubsection{Vertex Dependent $\eta$ Correction}
The transverse energy per pseudorapidity unit, \detdeta, is calculated in a given $\eta$ interval based on the $\eta$ position of individual calorimeter towers in the lab frame.
To take into account the position of the collision vertex, the $\eta$ value in each tower is corrected event-by-event relative to the vertex position of the collision. 
For the runs used in this analysis, the x and y vertex reconstruction was not possible due to absence of tracker detector data.
Therefore only a correction for the z-coordinate is used. 

In both simulation and data, a corrected tower $\eta$ is calculated for each tower as:
\begin{equation}
    \eta_{\text{corrected}} = \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{\text{tower}} - z_{\text{vertex}}}{\sqrt{x^2_{\text{tower}}+y^2_{\text{tower}}}}\right) = \sinh^{-1}\left(\frac{z_{\text{new}}}{r_{detector}}\right),
\end{equation}
where $x_{\text{tower}}$, $y_{\text{tower}}$, and $z_{\text{tower}}$ are the $x$, $y$, and $z$ positions of the towers with respect to the center of the detector, $z_{\text{vertex}}$ is the $z$ position of the vertex, and $r_{detector}$ is the radius of the detector subsystem in question (EMCal, IHCal, or OHCal).
The uncertainty in the event-by-event $\eta$ calculation due to no $x$ or $y$ vertex determination was found to be negligible for all calorimeters for $x$ or $y$ vertex shifts of up to 2 cm well beyond the nominal x and y vertex width of 0.01 cm.
A schematic of this event by event $\eta$ correction is given in Fig. \ref{fig:eta_determination}.

\begin{figure}
    \centering

    \caption{Schematic of event-by-event determination of calorimeter tower $\eta$ based on the reconstructed z vertex.}
    \label{fig:eta_determination}
\end{figure}

These event-by-event tower $\eta$ values are used in determining the tower $\ET$ values and in filling the reconstructed \detdeta histograms in both data and simulation, where $\ET = E_{\text{tower}}/\cosh\eta_{\text{corrected}}$ and $E_{\text{tower}}$ is the energy deposited in a given tower in data or simulation.

\subsection{Uncorrected and Reconstructed Simulated \detdeta}
To obtain uncorrected (reconstructed from data) or reconstructed simulated \detdeta for a given run and centrality class, $\ET$ is first calculated for each calorimeter tower as
\begin{equation}
    E_{\text{T,tower}}=E_{\text{tower}}\sin(\theta)=E_{\text{tower}}/\cosh\eta
\end{equation}
Tower $\ET$ are summed in $\eta$ across the full event selection and then scaled by the $\eta$ bin width and total number of events to obtain the average reconstructed \detdeta in a given $\eta$ interval per event.
In total, five reconstructed \detdeta measurements are made for each centrality bin: a reconstructed \detdeta from each of the individual calorimeters using only contributions from that calorimeters' towers (for example, the EMCal \detdeta measurement only has contributions from EMCal towers), a HCal reconstructed \detdeta histogram comprising both layers of the HCal, and a full calorimeter reconstructed \detdeta histogram, which is filled with tower contribution from all three calorimeter layers. 
The reconstructed full calorimeter measurement is the same as the sum of the reconstructed individual calorimeter measurements; 
there is no additional weighting applied to combine the contributions of the hadronic calorimeters and EM calorimeter to create the full calorimeter reconstructed \detdeta. 
Fig.~\ref{fig:dEtdeta_reco} shows the detector-level \detdeta measurements for all calorimeter layers for both data and simulation samples for the 0-5\% most central events; 
reconstructed \detdeta measurements for all centrality bins are included in Appendix~\ref{sec:full_detdeta_plots}.
For all centrality bins and calorimeter layer measurements, the simulation is able to reproduce the general shape and magnitude of the reconstructed \detdeta from data, indicating that the detector response and acceptance modeling performs well in the simulation.

\subsection{Truth \detdeta}
Truth \detdeta is calculated by binning $E_{\text{T,particle}}=E_{\text{particle}}/\cosh\eta$ as a function of $\eta$ and again correcting for the $\eta$ bin width. 
This histogram is then divided by the number of events in the centrality class in the same manner as the reconstructed \detdeta information. 

All particles within the detector's acceptance of $\eta$ < 1.1 are included and the truth \detdeta histogram is binned with the same bin sizes as the histogram of reconstructed \detdeta from calorimeter data. 
For baryons, the energy used for the truth \detdeta measurement is the particle's kinetic energy, for anti-baryons, the kinetic energy + twice the nucleon mass is used, and for all other particles, the total energy is used.

\subsection{Correction Factors}
Full simulation of the sPHENIX detector is performed to determine the \detdeta correction factors that take into account the acceptance and reconstruction efficiency.
The correction factors are derived from MC simulation and defined as the ratio of the transverse energy from reconstructed calorimeter towers to that in truth level for a given $\eta$ interval.
\begin{equation}
    C(\eta) = \frac{ \sum E_{\text{T,tower}}(\eta)}{ \sum E_{\text{T,particle}}(\eta)}
\end{equation}

Correction factors are created for each calorimeter measurement and each centrality bin using the mean simulated reconstructed \detdeta measurement over the truth transverse energy distribution for that centrality class. 
In this analysis, correction factors from the reweighted \epos dataset are used to correct the nominal \detdeta measurements, with correction factors from reweighted \ampt and \hijing used to determine the uncertainty in the MC reweighting method and residual model dependence. 
Correction factor distributions created using reweighted \epos, \ampt and \hijing for each calorimeter layer \detdeta measurement are shown in Figure~\ref{fig:correction_factors} for 0-5\% centrality events.
The correction factors for each calorimeter measurement are fairly consistent as a function of centrality with variations in the correction factors across all centrality bins studies in this analysis of at most 2-4\% for all calorimeter layer measurements.
The full set of correction factors for all centralities are also included in Appendix~\ref{sec:full_detdeta_plots}.

\subsubsection{Effect of MC Reweighting on Correction Factors}

The effect of the MC reweighting procedure on the correction factors used in the analysis was also studied by comparing the correction factors obtained from the reweighted \epos, \ampt and \hijing datasets to the correction factors obtained from the unweighted MC samples.
The effect of the MC reweighting procedure on the correction factors across the measurement's centrality range agrees well with the data/MC differences seen in the identified particle spectra ratios shown in Section~\ref{sec:detdeta_mcsamples}.
Noteably for both \epos and \ampt the agreement between data and MC in the particle spectra was best in very central events and poorer in more peripheral events. 
This trend can be seen in the correction factors as well, where the correction factors obtained from the reweighted and unweighted MC samples are more consistent in central events and less consistent in peripheral events, with the largest differences observed in the HCal correction factors for peripheral events. 
The \epos and \ampt correction factors were studied in detail here since they were seen as the best candidates for use in correcting the \detdeta measurements back to truth level. 
The \hijing correction factors were used to estimate the uncertainty in the correction factors from the MC reweighting procedure and to determine residual model dependence. More discussion on the MC and model dependence uncertainties is included in Section~\ref{sec:detdeta_systematics}.
A comparison of the correction factors obtained from the reweighted and unweighted MC samples for the EMCal, HCal and Full Calorimeter measurements for 0-5\% and 40-50\% centrality events is shown in Fig.~\ref{fig:correction_factors_reweighting}.
The full set of correction factors for all centrality bins for the reweighted and unweighted MC samples are included in Appendix~\ref{sec:full_detdeta_plots}.

\subsection{Corrected \detdeta}
Finally to obtain a fully corrected \detdeta measurement, the reconstructed \detdeta histogram from data is divided by the correction factors obtained from the reweighted \epos simulation. 
The correction factors account for non-uniform acceptance in the real detector by means of dividing out the reconstructed simulated \detdeta which has been artificially given the same acceptance as reconstructed data \detdeta.
Final results of the corrected \detdeta measurements are shown in Section~\ref{sec:detdeta_results} and the full set of corrected \detdeta measurements for all centrality bins are included in Appendix~\ref{sec:full_detdeta_plots}.
\begin{equation}
    \frac{\text{d}\ET}{\text{d}\eta}( \eta) = \frac{ \sum E_{\text{T,tower}}(\eta)}{C(\eta)}
\end{equation}
